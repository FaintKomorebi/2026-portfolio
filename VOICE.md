# Voice Brief · Noah Schilling

Copy direction for every word on this site. `DESIGN.md` governs how the site
looks. This governs how it sounds. Read both before writing or editing any copy.

Established 2026-08-19, from a full read-through of the live pages with Noah.

---

## The Target

Noah's own framing, kept verbatim because it is the most accurate description of
the target that exists:

> "I want to sound like I know my shit sure, but not shit covered with gold here
> and definitely not trying so hard to prove my work."
>
> "It should have some life and communicate what was done without fluff and
> without sounding like I'm 50 talking to other 50 year old product designers."

Two failure modes sit on either side of that line, and they are different problems:

1. **Consultant register.** Performing authority. Deck vocabulary, brand-voice
   sentences, claiming more rigor than the work actually had. This is the worse
   one and it is what `cs-acro.html` currently does.
2. **Claude rhythm.** "Not X but Y" reversals, aphoristic section closers,
   "actually" as a stress particle. Venial in a loose page, fatal when stacked.

A page can be clean on one and drenched in the other. Measure both separately.

## Rules

**Confidence comes from specificity, not vocabulary.** "29 pages and three clicks"
beats "a legacy brand experience." Numbers, page counts, and click counts carry
authority. Adjectives do not.

**Attribute every data claim, and never hedge a measured number.** "Maybe 90
seconds" reads as a rhetorical guess even when the figure came straight out of
Google Analytics. If it is measured, state it flat and tag the source the same way
the outcome stats already do (`Source · Google Analytics`). An unattributed
behavioral claim sitting next to a sourced one makes a reader quietly discount
both.

**Never write the client's brand copy.** A case study explains what Noah did. The
moment a sentence would fit on the client's own About page, it is out.

**Self-critique is quarantined.** Each case study gets exactly one designated place
to say what he would do differently. ACRO already has "What I'd Do Differently."
Nothing else on the page apologizes, hedges, or pre-concedes. Admitting fault
twice reads as lack of confidence, not honesty.

**Plain is not casual.** The register is direct, not informal. No profanity, no
"folks," no forced looseness. `about.html` is the ceiling and it is already at
the line.

**Banned vocabulary.** Deck nouns and agency verbs: legacy brand, entry point,
lens, closing the loop, holistic, strategic, leverage, elevate, seamless,
journey, unlock, pain points, entry point, "change management as design",
surface (as a verb, when it means "show"). Also: any sentence built on
"it's not X, it's Y" when another one is already nearby, and its dressed-up
cousin, "The mandate was X. The opportunity was Y."

**Allowed despite looking like hits:** "Surfaced independently in every
department" (`cs-sharepoint.html`) uses surface in the "emerged" sense, which is
fine. And "actually" is not banned, it is spoken register. Three or four across a
page is normal. Only worry when several land in one paragraph.

**No em dashes, anywhere, ever.** Periods, commas, colons, or a middle dot (·).
This is a global rule, not a voice preference.

## Approved Rewrites

Calibrated with Noah on 2026-08-19. These are the tuning fork. Match this
frequency, do not re-derive it.

**Hero**
- Was: "Redesigning a legacy brand for modern customers."
- Now: "ACRO has built automation systems for 90 years. It took 29 pages and three clicks to find that out."

**Lead**
- Was: "ACRO's 29-page website was burying a 90-year manufacturing reputation. A procurement manager landing on it had no fast way to confirm ACRO even served their industry. Most didn't stay long enough to find out."
- Now: "ACRO's 29-page site made buyers work for the basics. A procurement manager landing on it couldn't confirm ACRO served their industry without clicking into a landing page and scrolling to find the right item. Analytics showed most left before they got there." (plus source tag)

**Context open**
- Was: "ACRO had built something real over 90 years: a reputation for precision manufacturing that larger competitors couldn't match on track record alone. What it hadn't built was a way to communicate that."
- Now: "ACRO competes on track record, which is the one thing a bigger competitor can't buy. The site didn't lead with it. Competitors ran sites of 17 and 21 pages. ACRO ran 29, with its most-visited content sitting two clicks deep."

**Testing paragraph**
- Was: "internal review sessions with engineers and stakeholders who mirror the buyers evaluating ACRO... Every structural decision was tested against that lens before it was finalized."
- Now: "I ran the layouts past engineers and sales staff at ACRO, the people fielding buyer questions every day. Same question each time: if you landed here trying to find out whether ACRO can build what you need, does this page get you there?"

**Subheads**
- "Industry-led entry point" → "Sorted by industry, not product"
- "Consolidation over elimination" → "Fewer pages, same content"
- "Closing the loop" → "One screen, all three questions answered"

## The ACRO Dwell-Time Decision (2026-08-19)

The page used to claim "maybe 90 seconds to decide", "Most closed the tab", and
"Most didn't stay long enough to find out". All three were cut rather than
hardened, because the SEO provider's report gives April 2024 average session
duration as **1 second**, which is a tracking artifact, not a measurement. There
is no defensible absolute dwell figure for the pre-launch baseline.

What replaced them: the same claims stated as direction rather than magnitude,
attributed to analytics without a number ("the analytics showed most of them
leaving rather than digging for it"). Confident, and survives an interviewer
asking to see the report.

**Open risk, unresolved.** The +28% average session duration in Section 05 is
measured against that same pre-launch baseline. If the baseline really is logged
as 1 second, the percentage needs a different comparison window to mean anything.
Worth confirming with the third-party provider before this number gets defended
in an interview. Not changed on the page, because Noah stands behind the figure
and it predates this rewrite.
